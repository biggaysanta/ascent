# Text Neck Symptoms: Postural Degradation and Correction

## The Biomechanics of Modern Postural Strain

'Text neck' is a popularized term for a severe, modern biomechanical dysfunction: chronic forward-head posture induced by prolonged viewing of digital devices. In a neutral, structurally aligned position, the adult human head weighs approximately 10 to 12 pounds. The cervical spine's natural lordotic curve is perfectly engineered to distribute this weight evenly across the vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs.

However, as the head tilts forward to look down at a smartphone or tablet, the physics changes dramatically. At a 15-degree forward tilt, the effective weight placed on the cervical spine increases to roughly 27 pounds. At a 45-degree angle, it escalates to 49 pounds, and at a 60-degree tilt, the lower cervical spine must support an astonishing 60 pounds of structural load. This massive increase in force places immense stress on the posterior ligaments, cervical vertebrae, and muscles, initiating a predictable cascade of structural breakdown and tissue ischemia.

## The Structural Progression of Symptoms

The human body adapts dynamically to chronic forces. When subjected to hours of forward-head flexion daily, the musculoskeletal system undergoes a series of predictable, negative structural adaptations:

1.  **Ligamentous Creep:** Extended periods of forward bending cause the posterior longitudinal ligament and ligamentum flavum to lose their elastic snap, leading to micro-instability across cervical joint segments.
2.  **Upper Crossed Syndrome:** The nervous system automatically adapts to this habit by shortening the upper trapezius, levator scapulae, and pectoralis groups, while simultaneously inhibiting and weakening the deep cervical flexors (longus colli) and the lower rhoboids.
3.  **Chronic Base-of-Skull Pain:** The suboccipital muscles undergo continuous contraction to keep the eyes tracking horizontally, leading to severe localized tension. ### The McKenzie Cervical Retraction Drill
This foundational exercise helps restore proper vertical alignment. Sit upright with your chest open. Look straight ahead, and pull your chin smoothly straight back—as if avoiding a punch—keeping your eyes level. Do not tilt your head down; keep it on a horizontal plane. Hold the retracted position for 3 seconds, then release. Perform 10 repetitions every two hours during screen sessions to break the forward flexion habit.

### Conscious Ergonomic Shifts
When using a mobile device, raise your elbows to eye level rather than dropping your head to your lap. If you work in an office or study setting in the Sacramento area, position your primary computer monitors so that the top third of the screen sits exactly at eye level.
