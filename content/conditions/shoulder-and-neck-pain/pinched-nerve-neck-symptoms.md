# Pinched Nerve in Neck Symptoms: Identification and Care

## The Pathology of Cervical Radiculopathy

A 'pinched nerve' in the neck is the clinical equivalent of cervical radiculopathy. This condition arises when a spinal nerve root becomes mechanically compressed or chemically irritated as it exits the spinal cord through the intervertebral foramen. The root causes generally fall into two categories: mechanical compression from a herniated disc material or osteophytic encroachment (bone spurs) due to degenerative disc disease. 

When a nerve root is compressed, it does not merely cause a localized neck ache; it alters neural transmission along the entire pathway of that specific nerve path. This results in a distinct neurological presentation involving paresthesia (tingling), sharp electrical pain, sensory deficits, and localized muscle weakness across corresponding dermatomes and myotomes.

## Mapping Symptoms to Specific Vertebral Levels

Because each cervical nerve root innervates a precise territory of the upper extremity, clinicians can accurately map the source of the compression by tracking the distribution of the patient's symptoms.

| Nerve Root | Primary Compression Source | Sensation Pathway (Dermatome) | Motor Deficit / Weakness (Myotome) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **C5** | C4-C5 Disc Level | Lateral aspect of the upper arm over the deltoid muscle | Shoulder abduction (raising arm out to the side) |
| **C6** | C5-C6 Disc Level | Lateral forearm, radiating directly into the thumb and index finger | Biceps flexion and wrist extension |
| **C7** | C6-C7 Disc Level | Posterior arm, traveling down the middle finger | Triceps extension (straightening elbow) and wrist flexion |
| **C8** | C7-T1 Disc Level | Medial forearm, extending into the ring and little fingers | Finger flexion and grip strength integrity |

Recognizing these specific pathways is critical. If your pain tracks strictly down the lateral border of the arm into the thumb, you are likely looking at a C6 nerve root irritation. If your pain pattern is more localized to the upper torso without distal finger tingling, it is vital to cross-reference your symptoms with myofascial tracking guides like [neck and shoulder pain on one side](/neck-shoulder-pain-one-side) to ensure an accurate clinical picture.

## Clinical Signs and Diagnostic Testing

Identifying cervical radiculopathy involves specific physical assessments that alter the space inside the intervertebral foramen to replicate or alleviate symptoms.

### Spurling’s Test
This diagnostic maneuver is performed by gently extending the cervical spine, rotating the head toward the symptomatic side, and applying axial compression to the top of the head. If this maneuver recreates or intensifies the radiating, electrical pain down the patient's arm, it is a strong positive indicator of active nerve root compression within the neuroforamen.

### Cervical Distraction Test
Conversely, if a practitioner applies a gentle manual upward traction to the skull, expanding the intervertebral space, and the arm pain immediately diminishes, it confirms the presence of mechanical compression.

## Targeted Clinical Management Protocols

Conservative management for a confirmed pinched nerve focus heavily on reducing local chemical inflammation and restoring mechanical space to the nerve root.

1.  **Axial Cervical Traction:** Utilizing specialized manual traction allows the compressed disc spacing to temporarily widen, promoting the reabsorption of disc bulges and improving microcirculation to the ischemic nerve root.
2.  **Specific Neuromobilization:** Nerve flossing techniques can guide the nerve through its connective tissue pathway, breaking up subtle adhesions without triggering an inflammatory spike.
